Defines how HipMarvin FX research documents are reviewed, revised, and maintained.

This document governs editorial methodology only. It complements `STANDING_PROTOCOL.md`, which governs trading and research methodology, and `PUBLISHING_PIPELINE.md`, which governs transformation of research into publication.

This document applies equally to:

- Weekly Research File
- Daily Update File
- Week-End Review
- Position Ledger
- Standing Protocol
- Publishing Pipeline
- Research Templates
- Generation Prompts (the three Stage 1 prompt documents — Weekly Outlook, Daily Update, Week-End Review — as defined in `HipMarvinFX_Generation_Prompts_v4_9.md`)
- Future canonical documentation

**Scope note on Generation Prompts (added v1.1):** this Standard governs

If an editorial instruction conflicts with the Standing Protocol, the Standing Protocol takes precedence.

# EDITORIAL PRINCIPLES

## Principle 1 — Canonical revision, not reinterpretation.

Every edit produces the next canonical revision of the document.

Preserve as much existing text as possible.

Only modify text where there is a clear improvement supported by protocol, evidence, or analytical clarity.

Do not rewrite sections simply because they could be written differently.

---

## Principle 2 — Strengthen reasoning, never evidence.

Editorial revisions may improve:

- reasoning
- analytical depth
- logical flow
- clarity
- organization within existing fields
- cross-document continuity

Editorial revisions must never strengthen or weaken:

- evidence
- sourcing
- confidence
- probability
- verdict
- certainty

unless new evidence actually supports doing so.

Reasoning may become stronger.

Evidence may not.

---

## Principle 3 — Preserve uncertainty.

If evidence does not support a stronger conclusion, uncertainty remains visible.

Do not replace:

- appears
- likely
- probable
- unconfirmed
- pending

with stronger language unless supported by evidence.

Do not remove hedging simply to improve readability.

---

## Principle 4 — Remove ambiguity when evidence exists.

Where evidence clearly supports a conclusion, state it plainly.

Avoid unnecessary ambiguity.

Differentiate clearly between:

- price reaching an entry
- an executed position
- a confirmed trigger
- an inferred trigger

Do not leave uncertainty where the evidence already resolves it.

---

## Principle 5 — Preserve document continuity.

Every research document belongs to a larger research chain.

Weekly Research File
↓

Daily Update
↓

Week-End Review
↓

Position Ledger
↓

Publishing Pipeline

Revisions should maintain continuity between documents.

Where appropriate, reference earlier established analysis instead of treating every document as independent.

---

## Principle 6 — Carry forward active analytical context.

Analytical warnings remain active until they are genuinely resolved.

Examples include:

- Rule 5 Correlation Check
- Rule 21 Zone Alignment Check
- Thesis Invalidation Flag
- Carryover conflicts
- unresolved Ledger items

Do not silently remove these simply because price has moved.

Remove them only once the underlying condition has genuinely been resolved.

---

## Principle 7 — Respect document ownership.

Each document performs one defined job.

Weekly Research File develops the forward thesis.

Daily Update records what actually happened.

Week-End Review evaluates the completed week.

Position Ledger records position status.

Publishing Pipeline transforms research into publication.

Do not allow one document to perform another document's responsibility.

---

## Principle 8 — Preserve parser integrity.

Editorial revisions must not alter parser-visible structure.

Do not change:

- headings
- field names
- parser markers
- ordering
- schema
- confidence vocabulary

unless the governing template itself changes.

Standing Protocol Rule 20 always applies.

---

## Principle 9 — Cross-reference rather than duplicate.

If analysis already exists elsewhere in the research record, point to it.

Do not recreate it.

Examples:

"Per Week 32 Priority 2."

"Per Day 3 Verdict: Invalidated."

"Per Rule 21B."

The research record should contain one authoritative explanation rather than multiple independent versions.

---

## Principle 10 — Separate observation from interpretation.

Analytical writing should progress in three stages.

Observation

↓

Interpretation

↓

Conclusion

Do not skip directly from observation to conclusion.

Readers should always be able to see the reasoning chain.

---

## Principle 11 — Analysis before narrative.

Every revision must first improve analytical value.

Better writing is only an improvement when it also improves:

- analytical precision
- traceability
- decision quality
- protocol compliance

Narrative never replaces analysis.

---

# EDITORIAL REVIEW WORKFLOW

Every revision follows the same sequence.

## Step 1 — Protocol Compliance

Check against:

- Standing Protocol
- document template
- phase-specific rules

Resolve protocol conflicts before editing anything else.

---

## Step 2 — Evidence Review

Verify:

- prices
- forecasts
- actuals
- chart reads
- COT figures
- Ledger status

No evidence may be strengthened.

No evidence may be weakened.

---

## Step 3 — Reasoning Review

Improve:

- logic
- explanation
- causal links
- justification
- analytical depth

without changing conclusions unsupported by evidence.

---

## Step 4 — Continuity Review

Confirm consistency with:

- Weekly Research File
- Daily Updates
- Week-End Review
- Position Ledger
- Standing Protocol
- Publishing Pipeline

Carry unresolved issues forward where appropriate.

---

## Step 5 — Clarity Review

Reduce ambiguity where evidence supports doing so.

Preserve ambiguity where evidence does not.

---

## Step 6 — Canonical Revision

Produce the revised document directly.

Unless review-only is explicitly requested, return the updated document rather than editorial commentary.

---

# EDITORIAL DECISION HIERARCHY

When deciding whether to edit something, apply this order:

1. Standing Protocol
2. Editorial Standard
3. Document Template
4. Existing Canonical Document
5. New Evidence

Higher levels always override lower levels.

---

# DOCUMENT CONSISTENCY RULE

All HipMarvin FX documents should read as though they were written by one analyst over time.

Maintain consistency of:

- terminology
- analytical style
- confidence language
- document relationships
- reasoning patterns

Consistency is preferred over stylistic variation.

---

# AI EDITOR OPERATING MODE

Unless explicitly instructed otherwise, every HipMarvin FX document is treated as a canonical research record.

The editor must:

- produce the next canonical revision
- edit directly rather than only reviewing
- preserve structure
- preserve parser integrity
- preserve evidence hierarchy
- strengthen reasoning without changing evidence
- improve analytical clarity
- maintain cross-document continuity
- carry forward unresolved analytical context
- surface protocol conflicts rather than silently resolving them
- preserve uncertainty where evidence is incomplete
- remove ambiguity where evidence is sufficient
- return publication-ready text

---

# PROHIBITED EDITORIAL ACTIONS

The editor must never:

- fabricate evidence
- fabricate prices
- fabricate forecasts
- fabricate actuals
- fabricate trade outcomes
- silently resolve Ledger conflicts
- silently resolve Carryover conflicts
- silently upgrade confidence
- silently downgrade confidence
- rewrite documents for style alone
- alter parser-visible structure
- remove hedging without evidence
- introduce unsupported conclusions
- change the function of an existing document

---

# CHANGE CONTROL

This document governs editorial behaviour only.

Changes to research methodology belong in the Standing Protocol.

Changes to publication methodology belong in the Publishing Pipeline.

Changes to document schemas belong in their respective templates.

Editorial revisions must never be used to bypass those documents.

# RELATIONSHIP TO EXTERNAL REVIEW INTAKE (added v1.1)

`PUBLISHING_PIPELINE.md` defines its own "External Review Intake" section,
governing how externally-sourced recommendation documents are evaluated
before anything from them is accepted into the canonical set. That section
is narrower in scope than this Standard: it applies specifically to
recommendations arriving from outside the working session, not to
day-to-day maintenance, self-identified fixes, or routine revision of any
canonical file.

This Standard is the general procedure for revising any HipMarvin FX
canonical document, regardless of what prompted the revision. When
External Review Intake determines that an externally-sourced
recommendation should be acted on, the resulting edit — whether it lands
in `PUBLISHING_PIPELINE.md` or any other canonical file — is still
produced by following this Standard's Editorial Review Workflow (Steps
1–6) and Editorial Decision Hierarchy. External Review Intake decides
*whether* a recommendation should be acted on and by what reasoning;
this Standard governs *how* the resulting edit is actually made.

Neither document supersedes the other, and neither is duplicated inside
the other. External Review Intake's five steps stay in
`PUBLISHING_PIPELINE.md`, scoped to that document's own governance of
external input, consistent with the Document Consolidation Principle
already established there. This section exists only to state the
relationship explicitly, since the two were developed independently and,
prior to this revision, never cited each other despite covering
overlapping ground.
